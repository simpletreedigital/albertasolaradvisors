Bill Breakdown: How Solar Credits Slash Your Power Bill

If you're a homeowner in Alberta with solar panels, your electric bill likely looks different than it used to. One standout line item solar homeowners notice is a "Microgen" Credit, a key benefit of Alberta's net billing program that helps homeowners maximize their solar investment. Here's what each charge means and how solar dramatically reduces your monthly costs.

Charges Breakdown

Here's a simplified look at a typical real residential electricity bill for a solar homeowner in Edmonton who used 651 kWh in one billing cycle.

Energy$69.99
Regulated Transmission & Distribution$57.10
Municipal & Retailer Fees$14.07

Microgen Credit−$56.44
Final Bill Total$21.16

Microgen Credit: −$56.44. This is where the magic happens.

This homeowner exported excess solar energy back to the grid that the home didn't use, and their utility credited them $56.44. That's the essence of net billing in Alberta: your solar panels don't just power your home, they earn you credits when you produce more than you consume.

Final Bill Total: $21.16, an 85% reduction

That's an 85% reduction in electricity costs for this billing cycle, all thanks to solar and net billing. Because Alberta has ridiculously sunny summers, those export credits help offset the shorter days of winter, resulting in lower electric bills year-round.

This is why it's important to have an experienced solar company size your system correctly for your home's usage, so you aren't overpaying for a system that's too big, but you're still generating enough to cover what you need plus a little extra to earn credits.

What Is the Microgen Credit?

When your solar system produces more electricity than your home needs, the extra energy flows back into the grid. Your utility company tracks this and credits you for every kilowatt-hour exported. These credits directly reduce your bill.

In Alberta, this process is called net billing under the Micro-Generation Regulation, and it's one of the fastest ways to offset the cost of going solar.

How Net Billing Accelerates ROI

By generating excess power during sunny spring and summer months and banking credits, many homeowners:

  • Offset 60–90% of their annual power costs
  • See payback periods drop by 5–10 years depending on usage
  • Lock in long-term protection against rising utility rates
