Four Things Do Most of the Work

Most of the payback — and the winter peace of mind — comes down to these four.

Battery Backup

Store your summer surplus instead of losing it. When the grid blinks in a winter storm, your home doesn't — and you're still stacking credits the rest of the year.

Net Billing

Your panels send extra power to the grid in summer. That becomes a credit — sometimes called a Microgen Credit — applied straight to your bill. One real example: a $206.08 bill dropped to a ‑$11.72 credit.

The Solar Club™

Switch between a summer export rate and a winter import rate with 10 days' notice, no penalty. That rate arbitrage is what turns summer surplus into lower winter bills.

Financing

Depending on your city, you may qualify for CEIP (financed through your property tax bill) or a low-interest solar loan. Either way, your monthly payment is often lower than the savings — so the system can pay for itself from day one.

How the Solar Club Rate Switch Pays You All Winter

Two rates. You pick whichever one is working in your favor.

SeasonRateWhy
Summer (Mar–Oct)$0.30/kWhYou're exporting more than you use — switch to the high rate
Winter (Oct–Mar)$0.0877/kWhYou're importing more than you export — switch to the low rate

Example: 2,000 kWh exported in summer at the high rate earns about $600 in credits. 2,000 kWh imported in winter at the low rate costs about $175.40. Add a battery and you're using stored power instead of buying it back at all.
